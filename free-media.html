Free and Open Media

Where to find photos, video, music, sound effects, icons, fonts, 3D models, and data you can use for free, and what each site's license lets you do.

The rules behind these licenses (public domain, Creative Commons, fair use, and credit lines) are on Rights and Credit. This page is the list of places to look.

Editor note: sites change their licenses. Check every row against the site's current license page each summer, and note the date here.

Before you download

"Free" can mean three different things: the work is public domain, it has a Creative Commons license, or the site has its own license. Each one has different rules. For every item:

  1. Open the item's own page. Not the search result, a thumbnail, or a repost. One site can hold items under many licenses.
  2. Read the license on that page. If there isn't one, or it's unclear, pick something else.
  3. Check your use against it. Will you crop or edit it? Is the work commercial (client work, ads, or a news site that sells ads)? Does it show people, logos, or private property?
  4. Write down the credit now. Save the item's address, creator, title, license, and the date. See Credit lines.

Avoid NC (NonCommercial) material in published journalism and in any client work. A news site that sells ads counts as commercial.

Free stock photos and video

These sites use their own licenses, not Creative Commons. Credit isn't required, but give it anyway. Stock photos are staged or generic, so don't use them as documentary photos of a real event, place, or person in your story.

Some free stock sites now include AI-made images. Check the item page for an AI label, and don't use AI images where a reader would assume a real photo.

Fonts

Google Fonts are free for print, web, and apps. Most use the SIL Open Font License. Adobe Fonts come with the university's Adobe license, but only while you have it. See Font files and licenses on the Type page.

Facts aren't copyrighted, but datasets can come with terms of use. Credit the source of every dataset, even when you made the chart. How to check a dataset before you use it is on Research.

Quick answers

Do I need to credit an Unsplash or Pexels photo?
The license doesn't require it, but credit it anyway. Journalism expects a credit for every photo.
Can I use a CC BY-NC photo in my class project?
Only if the project won't be published. Once it's on a public site for a news outlet or a client, treat it as commercial and pick something else.
Is everything on Wikimedia Commons public domain?
No. Many files are Creative Commons and need a credit and a license link. The file page says which.
Can I use a free stock photo to show a real person or event in my story?
No. Stock photos are staged or generic. Use a photo of the real thing, with the right to use it.
Where do I put the credits?
Beside the item, or in a credits section on the page. See Credit lines.
