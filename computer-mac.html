Using a Mac

How to work fast on the Mac computers in SJMC labs: finding files, opening them in the right app, moving between apps, and the shortcuts professionals use every day.

Most people learn one way to do something on a computer and stop there. Professionals learn the faster way once and use it every day. A few shortcuts and habits save minutes on every project and keep you from losing work.

If you learned on a Chromebook, an iPad, or a phone, a Mac works differently in three ways:

  • Your files live in folders on the computer or a drive, not inside an app. See Files.
  • Closing a window doesn't quit the app. The app keeps running until you quit it.
  • A double-click opens a file in whatever app the Mac picks, which often isn't the app you need.

Menu paths on this page are for recent versions of macOS. Older versions put a few settings in different places.

Lab computers

You'll often sit at a different computer from one class to the next. Anything you set up on one lab computer isn't on the next one. That includes:

  • Hot corners and other System Settings
  • Which app opens a file type (Open With › Change All)
  • The apps and folders in the Dock
  • Workspaces and settings inside Photoshop, Premiere Pro, and other software. See Workspaces and settings.
  • Files saved to that computer's Desktop or Downloads folder

To come: whether lab computers erase your setup and files when you log out, or keep them on that one computer.

What to do about it:

  • Learn the keyboard shortcuts. They work the same on every Mac, with nothing to set up.
  • Open files from inside the app (File › Open) or drag them onto the app. Don't count on a double-click. See Opening a file in the right app.
  • Keep your work on your own drive or in the cloud, not on the lab computer. See Backups.
  • Set up the few things you rely on when you sit down, such as a hot corner or your workspace. It takes a minute.

On your own computer, set things up once and they stay.

Finding files and apps

Spotlight

Spotlight is the fastest way to open an app or find a file. Press ⌘-Space, type the first few letters of the name, and press Return. Type "phot" and press Return to open Photoshop. You don't need to find the app in the Dock or the Applications folder.

Where to find it: ⌘-Space, or the magnifying glass at the top right of the screen.

Finder

Finder is the app that shows your files and folders. It's always open: click the smiling face at the left end of the Dock to bring up a Finder window.

  • Sidebar: the list at the left of a Finder window. It shows your folders, drives you've plugged in, and cloud storage.
  • Path bar: shows the folders a file is inside, at the bottom of the window. Turn it on with View › Show Path Bar.
  • File extensions: turn them on so you can see whether a file is a .jpg, .png, or .psd. Choose Finder › Settings › Advanced and turn on Show all filename extensions. See File extensions.
  • Views: the buttons at the top of the window switch between icons, a list, columns, and a gallery. List view (⌘2) shows file size and date modified.

Quick Look

To check what a file is without opening any app, click it once and press the Space bar. Press Space again to close it. Quick Look shows images, PDFs, video, and audio. It's faster than opening Preview, and it doesn't open the wrong app.

Opening a file in the right app

When you double-click a file, the Mac opens it in the app it's set to use for that file type. On lab computers, images usually open in Preview, not Photoshop. Preview is fine for looking. It's not where you edit, and saving from Preview can change your file.

Open it in the app you want

  • From inside the app: in Photoshop, InDesign, or any other app, choose File › Open (⌘O) and find the file.
  • Drag it onto the app: drag the file onto the app's icon in the Dock.
  • Open With: Control-click (or right-click) the file and choose Open With, then the app.

These work on every computer, including lab computers.

Change the app for every file of that type

On your own computer, you can tell the Mac to open every file of a type in the app you choose.

  1. Click a file of that type once, such as a .png.
  2. Press ⌘I (File › Get Info).
  3. Under Open with, choose the app.
  4. Click Change All.

On a lab computer, this lasts only on that computer. See Lab computers.

Apps and windows

Closing a window isn't quitting

Clicking the red button at the top left of a window, or pressing ⌘W, closes the window. The app keeps running. A dot under its icon in the Dock shows it's still open. To quit, press ⌘Q or choose Quit from the app's menu (the menu named for the app, next to the Apple menu).

Quit apps you aren't using. Photoshop, Premiere Pro, and other professional software use a lot of memory even with no file open.

Switching between apps

  • ⌘Tab: hold ⌘ and press Tab to step through open apps. Let go to switch.
  • ⌘` (the key above Tab): switches between windows in the same app, such as two Photoshop files.
  • ⌘H: hides the app you're in so you can see what's behind it. It stays open.

When an app freezes

Press ⌥⌘⎋ (Option, Command, Escape) to open Force Quit, choose the app, and click Force Quit. You lose anything you didn't save, so save often (⌘S).

Desktops and Mission Control

A Mac can have more than one desktop, each with its own windows. Give each task its own desktop: Photoshop on one, ICON and the assignment sheet on another, Finder on a third. Switch between them instead of digging through stacked windows.

Mission Control shows every open window and every desktop at once. Click a window to go to it.

Hot corners

A hot corner runs an action when you push the pointer into a corner of the screen. You don't need a trackpad or a keyboard shortcut, so hot corners work well with a mouse. Common choices:

  • Mission Control: see every window and desktop
  • Desktop: slide all windows aside to get to files on the desktop
  • Application Windows: see every window of the app you're in
  • Lock Screen: lock the computer when you step away

Where to find it: Apple menu › System Settings › Desktop & Dock, then Hot Corners at the bottom of the page. Choose an action for each corner.

To keep from setting one off by accident, hold a modifier key such as ⌘ while you choose the action. Then the corner works only when you hold that key.

On a lab computer, hot corners last only on that computer. Setting one up takes under a minute.

Mouse and trackpad

Right-click

Right-click (or Control-click) opens a menu of what you can do with the thing you clicked: Open With, Rename, New Folder, and more. In professional software, right-click on a layer, clip, or object to see its options. Use it often.

Lab mice

The lab mice have no visible buttons. Click the right side of the mouse to right-click. If that doesn't work, Control-click.

If the lab mouse is an Apple Magic Mouse, it also takes a few gestures on its top surface:

  • Double-tap with two fingers: Mission Control
  • Swipe left or right with two fingers: move between desktops and full-screen apps
  • Swipe left or right with one finger: back and forward in a browser

Where to find it: Apple menu › System Settings › Mouse. Turn on Secondary click if right-click doesn't work, and see the gestures under More Gestures.

To come: which mouse the labs have, and whether it takes gestures.

With any mouse, use hot corners and the keyboard shortcuts for Mission Control and desktops. They do the same jobs as the gestures.

Trackpad

On a MacBook or with a Magic Trackpad, gestures replace most of these shortcuts:

  • Click or tap with two fingers: right-click
  • Swipe up with three fingers: Mission Control
  • Swipe left or right with three fingers: move between desktops and full-screen apps
  • Pinch with two fingers: zoom in and out in most apps
  • Spread your thumb and three fingers: show the desktop

Where to find it: Apple menu › System Settings › Trackpad. Each gesture has a short video showing how it works.

Quick answers

I double-clicked my image and it opened in Preview, not Photoshop.
The Mac opens images in Preview unless told otherwise. Quit Preview, then open the file from Photoshop with File › Open, or drag it onto the Photoshop icon in the Dock. See Opening a file in the right app.
I closed Photoshop but the computer is still slow.
You closed the window, not the app. Press ⌘Q to quit it. See Apps and windows.
My hot corners and settings are gone.
You're on a different lab computer. Setup stays on the computer where you made it. See Lab computers.
Where did my window go?
It may be on another desktop or behind other windows. Open Mission Control (⌃↑) to see every window. See Desktops and Mission Control.
Where did my download go?
The Downloads folder. In Finder, press ⌥⌘L. See Files.
How do I right-click with this mouse?
Click the right side of the mouse, or hold Control and click. See Mouse and trackpad.
